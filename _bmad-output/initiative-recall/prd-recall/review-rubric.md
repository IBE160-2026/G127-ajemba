# PRD Quality Review — Recall

## Overall verdict
This is a strong, honest solo-project PRD. Its thesis ("the student can check the AI instead of trusting it") runs through the Vision, the Source Reference mechanism, the §5 quality section and the metrics, and most FRs carry real testable consequences. Scope and assumptions are handled unusually transparently (§11, §7, §8.2). The main risks are in the part the lecturer cares about most: the Essay Feedback evaluation has a design gap (AI-generated essay questions versus fixed prepared answers), UJ-1 contradicts the FR-6 performance bound, and several §5 checks cannot actually measure what they claim to (answer-key correctness, language-error insensitivity, zero-tolerance faithfulness). Cosmetic and mechanical drift (FR order in §5, glossary variants, leftover lowercase tag fragments) is minor.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Decisions are stated as decisions and logged: §11 records every assumption with its confirmation and the one change (faithfulness target tightened). Trade-offs are named with what was given up: no readability formula (FR-8), no admin screen (FR-19), no partial Summary (FR-5), builder-only judgement acknowledged as a limit in §5.7. Open Questions are real (stack, Test Set lectures, deadline).

The weaker spot is where a decision was made too bluntly and the consequences were not surfaced. "No unsupported statement in any Summary in the Test Set" (FR-25, SM-9) was deliberately tightened from "most Summaries" for measurability, but the PRD never admits that an LLM with zero tolerance will likely fail this target, and that FR-27 then forces rollbacks of otherwise good prompt changes. Open Question 5 (question counts) and the FR-4 threshold appear both as "confirmed" in §11 and as open in §10, which blurs what is actually settled.

### Findings
- **medium** Zero-tolerance faithfulness target with an undefined judge (FR-25 "Summary faithfulness"; SM-9) — "No unsupported statement in any Summary" is binary, depends on one reader (the builder) and does not define "unsupported" (paraphrase? inference? omission?). Combined with FR-27 ("not kept if it breaks a check that passed before") it can stall prompt work. *Fix:* define "unsupported" (contradicts or adds a claim not on the cited slides), and state what happens if the target cannot be met (record and explain, as the PRD already does for percentages).
- **low** Settled versus open is unclear (§10 Q4, Q5 versus §11) — Q4 and Q5 are open "to be tuned" yet also listed as confirmed. *Fix:* say in §10 "confirmed starting value, tuned after first runs".

## Substance over theater — strong
One persona (Amara) who drives concrete decisions (second language, plain language, no grammar criticism, Source References). The Vision admits "There is no technical moat", which is the opposite of innovation theater. NFRs carry thresholds (50 pages / 10 MB, 60 s, 2 s, 5 uploads/day, 100 characters/page). The only furniture-leaning item is §6.3 "WCAG 2.1 AA ... no formal audit", which is a target with no check (see Done-ness).

### Findings
- None beyond §6.3 (covered under Done-ness).

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is clear and the feature set follows it. The build order ("Summary, Multiple Choice, Fill-in-the-Gaps, Essay Feedback ... most time is left for Essay Feedback") shows prioritisation logic. Counter-metrics SM-C1 to SM-C3 exist, which is rare. However, all ten SMs are functional/quality test cases. None tests the thesis itself: that second-language students find the plain-language Summary easier, or the Vision's "in about fifteen minutes knows what they understood". Plain language is judged only by the builder (FR-8), so the primary user's core need is never checked by anyone who shares that need. The counter-metrics have no measurement method.

### Findings
- **medium** Metrics do not validate the thesis or the 15-minute claim (§1 "in about fifteen minutes"; UJ-1 "about fifteen minutes"; §9) — nothing measures time-to-insight or plain-language fit for a second-language reader. *Fix:* add one cheap check, for example a timed walkthrough of UJ-1 by one or two non-native-English students, or explicitly list this in §5.7 / §9 as untested.
- **low** Counter-metrics are not measurable (SM-C1 "Number of questions generated", SM-C2 "Quiz score", SM-C3 "Summary length") — they say "do not optimize" but give no way to see whether they were violated. *Fix:* attach a reading, for example "report the number of duplicate or wrong questions found in the evaluation report".

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs are good: FR-1/2/3/10/12/16/17/20/21 have concrete, falsifiable consequences, and FR-12 includes worked examples. The weaknesses concentrate where the AI is involved, which is exactly where the lecturer's §5 requirement bites.

### Findings
- **high** Essay evaluation design gap: AI-generated essay questions versus fixed prepared answers (FR-13, FR-24, FR-25, FR-26, Glossary "Reference Key Point") — FR-13 has the AI generate Essay questions with their Reference Key Points, and FR-25 sends the Test Set "through the real AI". FR-26 then needs prepared answers "to each Essay question" "marked in advance by the builder with the Reference Key Points it covers". If the essay question is regenerated per run, the prepared answers and their pre-marked key points do not match it. The Glossary also says Reference Key Points are "written down by the builder before any AI output is generated", which contradicts FR-13's AI-generated key points. *Fix:* state that Test Set essay questions and their key points are builder-written and frozen, with the evaluation calling only the assessment step; or state that the first AI-generated essay questions are frozen and committed before answers are written.
- **high** UJ-1 contradicts FR-6 (UJ-1 "40-slide databases lecture"; "Within a minute she sees a plain-language summary"; FR-6 "up to 30 pages ... under 60 seconds"; FR-3 allows up to 50 pages) — the only user journey uses a lecture outside the one size with a performance guarantee, and pages 31 to 50 have no performance bound at all. *Fix:* change UJ-1 to a lecture of 30 slides or fewer, or raise the FR-6 bound to 50 pages, or add a bound for 31 to 50 pages (for example a progress indicator and under 120 s).
- **medium** Correctness of answer keys is never checked (FR-9, FR-11, FR-25 "Question grounding") — the checks verify structure (four options, one correct), grounding ("based on a Reference Key Point or clearly on the slide text") and that exactly one option is marked correct, but not that the marked option or expected term is actually right. A well-grounded question with a wrong key is the worst failure for a student and passes every listed check. *Fix:* add to the Question checks "the builder verifies the marked correct answer against the slides for a sample of at least N questions per Lecture, target 100 % correct keys".
- **medium** Language-error insensitivity is a requirement but not tested (FR-14 bullet 2; FR-26) — "Language errors do not lower the Essay Rating" is central to the second-language user, yet the prepared answers are only good, weak and misunderstanding. No answer isolates spelling and grammar errors. The Test Set also does not require a Norwegian-language lecture although §4.3 supports Norwegian slides. *Fix:* add a prepared answer category "good content with many language errors" that must rate equal to the clean good answer, and require one test Lecture in Norwegian or state it is untested.
- **medium** Essay Rating is too coarse for strict ranking (Glossary "Essay Rating"; FR-13 "at least two Reference Key Points"; FR-26 "higher ... than every known weak answer") — with two key points the rating takes the values 0, 1 or 2, so ties between "good" and "weak" answers are likely and the 3x repeat rule becomes hard to meet. The rating is also an AI output compared with ranking only, while the builder has pre-marked coverage that could be compared exactly. *Fix:* require three or more Reference Key Points per Essay question, and add an exact-match comparison (AI-reported covered points versus builder-marked) as a check.
- **low** Vague or missing bounds (FR-6 and §6.4 "normal connection"; FR-14 "without an unusable error"; §6.3 "major browsers" and "WCAG 2.1 AA ... no formal audit"; FR-11 and FR-13 have no generation-time bound while FR-9 has one) — *Fix:* name a test condition ("on the university network"), a list of browsers, and a lightweight accessibility check (for example an automated axe/Lighthouse run with score threshold).

## Scope honesty — strong
Non-Goals (§7), Out of Scope (§8.2, FR-5), "If time" ordering, Non-Users (§2.2) and the Limits section (§5.7) make omissions explicit, and §5.7 is a model of honesty about what the Test Set does not prove. Open-items density is low for a solo course project. The inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags were deliberately removed in favour of §11, so a tag-to-index roundtrip is not applicable; the §11 list is complete for the choices visible in the body.

### Findings
- **low** Cross-cutting commitments without an owner or test (§6.1 "one-time notice ... must be acknowledged on first upload"; §6.2 "5 uploads per User per day") — these are concrete behaviours but have no FR ID, no consequences and appear in no SM or in §8.1 scope. Downstream story creation will miss them. *Fix:* promote both to FRs with consequences, or add them to the §8.1 list.

## Downstream usability — adequate
Glossary is thorough and mostly used verbatim; FR IDs 1 to 29 are contiguous, unique and every cross-reference resolves (see Mechanical notes). Weaknesses: UJ-1 is the only journey, and every feature section says "Realizes UJ-1", including ones it cannot realise.

### Findings
- **low** UJ coverage is thin and the traceability is partly nominal (§2.3; §4.9 "Reporting Wrong Questions" has no "Realizes" line; §4.10 "Realizes UJ-1 with prepared data"; §4.8 FR-18 delete) — reporting a question, deleting a lecture and the grader running Demo Mode are not in any journey. *Fix:* add a short UJ-2 for the grader/Demo Mode path (the grader is a stated reader in §0) or state that these are operational features without a journey.
- **low** SMs omit parts of the FRs they claim to validate (SM-5 "Validates FR-14, FR-26" but states only good-over-weak and weak-names-missing-point, omitting the misunderstanding rule and the three-run stability; SM-9 lists three targets but omits the 100 % exactly-one-answer and 90 % source-reference spot check in FR-25; SM-8 bundles four unrelated checks) — a reader of §9 alone will under-test. *Fix:* make SM-5 and SM-9 mirror FR-26 and FR-25 fully, or reference them as "all consequences of".

## Shape fit — strong
A solo, graded, consumer-style web app with one persona: a single named-protagonist UJ plus a capability-style FR list is the right shape. Rigor is heavier than a typical solo project (29 FRs, 13 metrics, a full §11 log) but is justified by the lecturer's explicit asks for a §5 and testable criteria. §11 is long and duplicates §0 to §10; acceptable but it adds reading load for downstream steps.

### Findings
- None.
